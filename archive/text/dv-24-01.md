# Prison overcrowding in Italy: a map of 189 prisons


# Visualising Prison Overcrowding in Italy

A Demers cartogram of all 189 Italian prisons, comparing the people held with the places available

Italian prisons hold more people than they have room for. This poster shows where, one institution at a time. I made it as the final project of the Executive course in Data Visualization at POLI.design, during the closing in-person workshop.


## Two sources, one dataset

The data comes from the Ministry of Justice’s Detenuti presenti, updated to 31 May 2024, and from Nodo alla gola, the twentieth report on detention conditions published by Antigone in 2024. The two sources name the same prisons in different, often abbreviated forms. Much of the work went into cleaning those names so that each prison could be matched across the datasets.


## A map made of prisons

The map is a Demers cartogram: each of the 189 prisons is a square placed close to its real position, without overlapping the others, so together they form a loose map of Italy. A solid square shows the number of people held; a dotted square shows the regulatory capacity. Wherever the solid square outgrows the dotted one, the prison is overcrowded, and across the map that happens again and again. Colour distinguishes eight types of institution, from district jails to prisons for women and the ICAM units for mothers with children.


## Three numbers as areas

Three figures sit beside the map, each drawn as an area at the map’s scale:

- 10,130 places missing nationwide: the gap between regulatory capacity and the people held, counting 9 m² for a single prisoner and 5 m² for each additional one, as the Ministry does.
- 3,640 places that the Ministry itself lists as unavailable inside prisons already in use.
- 17,347 people, 28.3% of all prisoners, who, according to Antigone, live in cells with less than 3 m² of floor space each.
